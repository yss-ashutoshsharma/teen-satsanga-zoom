Read this firstButton names and positions change a little between Zoom versions, so a label on your screen may be slightly different from the one written here. The place you look (the toolbar at the bottom of the meeting window) stays the same. If you don't see a button, click More (the three dots) at the right end of the toolbar.

How to read this document

  • Button — a button or menu item exactly as it is written on your Zoom screen. Look for these words.
  • Share / End — buttons that are green or red on screen. Red ones end or leave something, so read them twice.
  • Alt + A — keys to press together on the keyboard. ⌘ Cmd is the Command key on a Mac.
  • Participants → More → Rename — an arrow means "then click". Follow left to right.
  • Numbered circles — steps to do in order. Bullets — things to know, in no particular order.
  • Windows Mac — side-by-side boxes appear only where the two differ. If there is no box, the steps are the same on both.
  • Yellow box a helpful tip. Red box something that commonly goes wrong. Green box a recommended practice. Teal box important background.
  • "Host", "co-host" and "participant" are Zoom's own words for roles. Section 1 explains what each can do.

1. Host, co-host and participant Zoom gives each person a role. The role decides which buttons you see.

RoleWho it isWhat it can do
HostThe person who started (or owns) the meeting.Everything: admit people from the waiting room, mute and unmute, rename, remove, lock the meeting, manage breakout rooms and security settings, end the meeting for all.
Co-hostAnyone the host promotes.Almost everything the host can do (mute, admit, rename, manage breakout rooms, share screen). A co-host cannot end the meeting for all or remove the host, and cannot make other co-hosts.
ParticipantEveryone else.Mute and unmute themselves, switch video on and off, raise a hand, use reactions and chat, and move in and out of breakout rooms, within what the host allows.

Make someone a co-host

  1. Click Participants on the bottom toolbar.
  2. Hover over the person's name and click More.
  3. Click Make Co-host. To take it back, use More → Withdraw Co-host Permission.

Always have a second person with host powersIf the host's internet drops, a co-host can keep the meeting running. If there is no co-host, Zoom picks a new host automatically after a short wait, which may not be the person you wanted.

2. Update Zoom Do this a day before your session, not five minutes before.

An old version of Zoom may be missing buttons, may refuse to join, or may behave differently from what other people see. Updating takes two to three minutes.

Windows Check for updates

  1. Open the Zoom app (blue camera icon) and sign in.
  2. Click your profile picture or initials in the top-right corner.
  3. Click Check for Updates.
  4. If an update is found, click Update and wait. Zoom restarts by itself.

Mac Check for updates

  1. Open the Zoom app and sign in.
  2. Click your profile picture or initials in the top-right corner, then Check for Updates.
    Or use the menu bar at the very top of the screen: zoom.us → Check for Updates.
  3. Click Update. If asked, choose "Install for me only" and enter your Mac password.

Switch on automatic updates (recommended)

  1. Click your profile picture, then Settings.
  2. In the General tab, find "Zoom updates".
  3. Tick Automatically keep Zoom desktop client up to date. Leave the frequency on "Slow" — it is the stable option.

If "Check for Updates" is missingYou are probably using Zoom in a web browser, not the installed app. Close the browser tab and open the Zoom app from your Start menu (Windows) or Applications folder (Mac). If it is genuinely not installed, download it from zoom.us/download and choose "Zoom Workplace" (the desktop app).

3. Before every session A ten-minute routine that prevents most problems.

  1. Join 10–15 minutes early. You can test everything while the room is still empty.
  2. Test your microphone and speaker. Click the small arrow ^ next to Mute → Audio Settings… → use "Test Speaker" and "Test Mic".
  3. Check your camera. Click the arrow next to Start Video and make sure the correct camera is ticked.
  4. Close other apps that use the camera (Teams, FaceTime, Skype, a browser tab with a video call). Only one app can use the camera at a time.
  5. Plug in your laptop. Video calls drain batteries fast.
  6. Have the meeting link and the host key or passcode written somewhere outside Zoom (a notebook, a text file) in case you get disconnected.
  7. Mute notifications on your computer so messages don't pop up while you share your screen.

Windows Camera or mic blocked?

Open Settings → Privacy & security → Camera (and Microphone). Make sure access is On, and that Zoom is allowed in the list.

Mac Camera or mic blocked?

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera (and Microphone). Switch Zoom on. You may need to quit and reopen Zoom.

4. Rename yourself Use a name other people will recognise, for example your first name and surname.

Works the same on Windows and Mac. Pick whichever way is easiest for you.

Way 1 — from your own video tile

  1. Hover your mouse over your own video tile (or your name if your video is off).
  2. Click the three dots … in the corner of the tile.
  3. Click Rename, type the new name, and click Change (or Rename).

Way 2 — from the Participants panel

  1. Click Participants on the bottom toolbar. A list opens on the right.
  2. Hover over your name, click More → Rename.
  3. Type the name and confirm.

Set your name before you join

When you click a meeting link, Zoom shows a box asking for "Your Name" before you enter. What you type there is what everyone sees. If you are signed in, change the permanent name in Settings → Profile instead.

As host, you can rename anyoneIn the Participants panel, hover over any name → More → Rename. Useful when someone joins as "iPad" or "Dad's laptop" and you need to know who they are.

5. Turn your video on and off

  1. Look at the bottom-left of the meeting window for the camera button. It says Start Video (video is off) or Stop Video (video is on).
  2. Click it once to switch. A red line through the camera icon means your video is off.
Keyboard shortcutWindowsMac
Start / stop videoAlt + V⌘ Cmd + Shift + V

Set or change your profile picture

When your video is off, Zoom shows your profile picture on your tile instead (or your initials if you haven't set one). Set it once and Zoom remembers it.

  1. Save the picture you want on your computer (a JPG or PNG; a square crop looks best).
  2. Open the Zoom app and sign in. Click your profile picture or initials in the top-right corner, then click Settings.
  3. Click Profile on the left, then click your current picture (or the Edit / pencil icon next to it).
  4. Choose Change Picture (on some versions this opens zoom.us in your browser — sign in there and click Change under the picture).
  5. Select the saved picture, crop it so the subject is centred, and click Save.
  6. To check it, start a meeting on your own (Zoom app → New Meeting) with video off and look at your tile.

Windows

Pictures are usually in Downloads or Pictures. In the file picker, click the file once and then Open.

Mac

A picture saved in Photos must first be exported: in Photos, select the image → File → Export → save to Desktop. Then choose it in Zoom.

Changing it during a meetingHover over your tile → … → Edit Profile Picture. Handy if you notice the wrong picture after joining.

A few things that help with video

  • If the picture is dark, sit facing a window or lamp rather than with it behind you.
  • To hide your own face from your screen (not from others), hover over your tile → … → Hide Self View. Some people find this less distracting.
  • The arrow ^ next to the video button lets you choose a different camera or blur your background (Choose Virtual Background… / Blur).

6. Mute and unmute The most used button in any session.

  1. The microphone button is at the far bottom-left. It says Mute (you are live) or Unmute (you are silent).
  2. Click once to switch. A red line through the microphone means you are muted.
  3. Hold the spacebar to talk briefly while muted — like a walkie-talkie. Release it and you are muted again. (Works when the Zoom window is active.)
Keyboard shortcutWindowsMac
Mute / unmute yourselfAlt + A⌘ Cmd + Shift + A
Mute everyone (host)Alt + M⌘ Cmd + Ctrl + M

Muting the participants (host)

  1. Open Participants.
  2. Click Mute All at the bottom of the panel.
  3. A box asks whether to "Allow participants to unmute themselves". Untick it if you want to control who speaks; tick it for free discussion.
  4. To unmute one participant, hover over their name → Ask to Unmute. Zoom cannot force a participant's microphone on — they must click "Unmute" on their side. Say their name out loud and wait.

A simple routine for larger meetingsEveryone muted by default. People raise a hand to speak (Section 7). The host calls their name, they unmute, speak, and mute again. Explain this in the first minute and it becomes automatic.

7. Raise hands and reactions Emojis are the quietest way for people to respond.

Raise and lower your hand

  1. Click React (also shown as Reactions) on the bottom toolbar.
  2. Click Raise Hand. A small hand appears on your video tile and next to your name in the Participants list.
  3. To lower it, click React again → Lower Hand.
Keyboard shortcutWindowsMac
Raise / lower handAlt + YOption + Y

Emojis and quick answers

The same React menu has a row of emojis (clap, heart, laugh, surprised, thumbs up, celebrate) plus quick responses such as Yes, No, Slower, Faster. An emoji shows on the participant's tile for about ten seconds and then disappears. Click … in the menu to see every emoji.

Seeing and managing raised hands (host)

  • Raised hands appear in the order they were raised at the top of the Participants list — so you always know who was first.
  • To lower one participant's hand: hover their name → Lower Hand.
  • To clear everyone: at the bottom of Participants click … → Lower All Hands.
  • Hands stay up until lowered, and people often forget. Lower them yourself after the person has spoken.

Ways to use reactions in a meeting"Thumbs up if you can hear me." "Clap when you have finished." "Raise your hand if you want to answer." Reactions keep a meeting quiet while still letting everyone respond.

8. Using chat Helpful for links and instructions; distracting if left wide open.

Open chat and send a message

  1. Click Chat on the bottom toolbar. A panel opens on the right.
  2. At the bottom, check the "To:" box says Everyone.
  3. Type your message and press Enter. Press Shift + Enter for a new line without sending.
  4. Shortcut to open chat: Alt + H on Windows, ⌘ Cmd + Shift + H on Mac.

Private (direct) messages

Zoom lets anyone send a private "direct message" to a single person. Other people in the meeting cannot see it. Private messages are allowed by default, so a host who wants chat limited to the whole group, or to hosts only, has to change the setting. The steps are below and in Section 10.

Turn off private chat for everyone (host)

  1. Open Chat.
  2. Click the … (three dots) at the bottom of the chat panel.
  3. Under "Participants can chat with", choose Hosts and co-hosts so participants can message only the host and co-hosts, or Everyone if you want group chat that everybody can read. Never choose "Everyone and anyone directly", which allows one-to-one chat. Full steps are in Section 10.

Before you sendLook at the "To:" box every time. If it shows a participant's name instead of "Everyone", your message is private. Change it back to Everyone.

Save the chat

Click … at the bottom of the chat panel → Save Chat. Zoom saves a text file in your Documents → Zoom folder. Do this at the end of each session so you have a record.

9. Breakout rooms Small-group time. This is the section people worry about most, so it is the most detailed.

The one thing to rememberIn a breakout room there are two different red buttons. Leave Room takes you back to the main session. Leave Meeting ends your call completely. Always read the button before you click it.

A. Entering a breakout room (as a participant)

  1. When the host opens the rooms, a box appears: "The host is inviting you to join Breakout Room: Room 1". Click Join.
  2. If the box disappeared, click Breakout Rooms on the toolbar and then Join next to your room.
  3. You will briefly see "Joining Breakout Room…". Your audio and video settings stay exactly as they were.
  4. If the host set rooms to open automatically, you simply move — nothing to click.

B. Returning to the main room (without leaving the call)

  1. Click Leave Room at the bottom-right of the window.
  2. A small menu appears. Click Leave Breakout Room. Do not click "Leave Meeting".
  3. You are back in the main room with the host. (If the host has disabled this, you'll see "Ask for Help" instead — use it to call the host.)

C. Going back into your breakout room

  1. While rooms are still open, click Breakout Rooms on the toolbar.
  2. Click Join next to your room (or "Join Breakout Room").

D. If you were moved out, dropped, or lost connection

  1. Click the original meeting link again (or open Zoom → Join and enter the meeting ID). Use the same name as before.
  2. If there is a waiting room, wait — the host will admit you. Say nothing; hosts check the waiting room regularly.
  3. You arrive in the main room, not your breakout room. Click Breakout Rooms → Join, or wait for the host to reassign you.
  4. If you cannot see a Breakout Rooms button, send one chat message to Everyone: "Back in main room, please send me to Room 2."

E. When the host closes the rooms

A message says "Breakout Rooms will close in 60 seconds". You can click Return to Main Session straight away, or finish your sentence — Zoom moves everyone back automatically when the timer ends.

F. Asking the host for help from inside a room

Click Ask for Help on the toolbar → Invite Host. The host gets a notification and can join your room. There is also a chat inside each room — messages there go only to the people in that room.

G. Creating and running breakout rooms (as host)

  1. Click Breakout Rooms on the toolbar. (If it's missing, enable it at zoom.us → Settings → In Meeting (Advanced) → Breakout room, before the session.)
  2. Choose how many rooms, then Assign automatically (Zoom splits the group), Assign manually (you choose), or Let participants choose room.
  3. Click Create. You can rename a room (hover → Rename) and drag names between rooms.
  4. Click Options (gear) and consider ticking:
    • Automatically move all assigned participants into breakout rooms — participants don't need to click Join.
    • Allow participants to return to the main session at any time — tick it so nobody feels trapped.
    • Breakout rooms close automatically after — set a time such as 10 minutes.
    • Countdown after closing — 60 seconds is kind; 10 seconds is abrupt.
  5. Click Open All Rooms.
  6. While rooms are open you can Join any room to visit, Broadcast a message to every room (for example "2 minutes left"), or move a participant between rooms (hover the name → Move To).
  7. Click Close All Rooms to bring everyone back. You stay in the main room throughout unless you choose to visit.

Co-hosts can help in roomsBefore opening rooms, make your helpers Co-hosts (Participants → hover name → More → Make Co-host). A host can be in only one room at a time, so a co-host in another room can manage that room and ask for help if needed.

10. Host controls for a calm session Settings that keep a meeting secure and tidy.

The Security button

Click Security (shield icon) on the toolbar. This menu controls what participants can do:

OptionSuggested settingWhy
Enable Waiting RoomOnYou approve each person before they enter. Nobody unexpected gets in.
Lock MeetingOn, 5 minutes after startNobody else can join at all, even with the link. Unlock if a participant reconnects.
Share ScreenOff for participantsOnly you and co-hosts can share. Prevents surprises.
ChatOn, but limited (see below)Needed for links and instructions; one-to-one chat must be off.
Annotate on Shared ContentOffStops participants drawing over whatever is being shared.
Rename ThemselvesOff after the first few minutesStops silly names mid-session.
Unmute ThemselvesYour choiceOff = you control who speaks. On = freer discussion.
Start VideoOnLets participants switch video on when they're ready.

Chat: disable one-to-one chat and allow chat with hosts only

Do this before the first participant is admitted. It takes thirty seconds.

  1. Click Chat on the toolbar to open the chat panel.
  2. Click … (three dots) at the bottom of the chat panel, next to the message box.
  3. Under "Participants can chat with", choose Hosts and co-hosts. Participants can now send a message only to the host and co-hosts. Nobody can send a private message to another participant or to a single person.
  4. If you want everyone to read each other's messages as well, choose Everyone instead. Never choose "Everyone and anyone directly": that is the setting that allows one-to-one chat.
  5. Messages you send still go to Everyone, so participants can read your instructions. Check the "To:" box each time you type.

Make it the default, so you don't have to rememberSign in at zoom.us → Settings → In Meeting (Basic) → find "Meeting chat" → set "Allow participants to chat with" to Hosts and co-hosts (or Everyone) and untick Allow users to chat with others directly (the wording varies: "private chat" or "1:1 chat"). Every meeting you start will then begin with one-to-one chat already off.

Disable annotation on shared screens

When anyone shares a screen, participants can draw on it unless annotation is switched off. In a large group, someone drawing over your slides is a common disruption.

  1. During a meeting, from the Security menu: click Security → under "Allow participants to", untick Annotate on Shared Content. This applies to every share in the meeting.
  2. While you are sharing, as a backup: on the top toolbar click More → Disable Annotation for Others. You can still annotate yourself.
  3. For good, at zoom.us: Settings → In Meeting (Basic) → find "Annotation" and switch it off, or keep it on and tick "Only the user who is sharing can annotate". If your organisation's admin has locked this setting you will see a padlock — ask them to change it.

Suggested combinationAnnotation off for participants, chat set to Hosts and co-hosts, participant screen share off, waiting room on. Set these once at zoom.us and they apply to every meeting you start.

Waiting room

When someone arrives you'll see a notification and a number on the Participants button. Open Participants → under "Waiting Room" click Admit next to the name, or Admit All. If a name doesn't match your list, do not admit them.

Removing someone

Participants → hover the name → More → Remove. They cannot rejoin that meeting. Use "Put in Waiting Room" instead if you just need a pause.

Spotlight — make one video big for everyone

Hover a tile → … → Spotlight for Everyone. Participants see that person large, no matter who is speaking. Spotlight yourself while you teach; spotlight a participant while they present. Remove with the same menu → Remove Spotlight.

Make a helper Co-host

Participants → hover name → More → Make Co-host. Co-hosts can mute, admit, manage breakout rooms and keep the session going if you drop. Always have at least one.

Ending the session

Click End at the bottom-right → End Meeting for All. If you only click "Leave Meeting", the participants stay in the room without you — Zoom will ask you to pick a new host first. Say goodbye, wait for the participants to leave, then end for all.

11. Sharing your screen — basic and advanced Show slides, a document, a video or a sound clip.

Basic: share one window

  1. Open the thing you want to show before you click share.
  2. Click Share (or Share Screen) in the centre of the toolbar.
  3. In the box that opens, stay on the Basic tab and choose one window (for example "Slides.pdf") rather than "Desktop/Screen 1". Participants then see only that window, never your email, WhatsApp or notifications.
  4. Playing a video or music? Tick Share sound and Optimize for video clip at the bottom of the share box before clicking Share.
  5. Click Share. A green border shows what is being shared. Zoom's toolbar moves to the top of your screen.
  6. To stop, click Stop Share at the top.

While you are sharing

  • Hover over the top toolbar to see Pause Share (freezes what the participants see while you find the next file), Annotate (draw or highlight) and More.
  • Share and stay visible: your own video tile stays on screen for the participants; to see your video and the participants' faces while sharing, drag the small floating video panel to a corner, or click View → Gallery in that panel.
  • Switch to another window without stopping: click New Share on the top toolbar and pick the next window. There is no gap for the participants.
  • If you need to use the chat or participants list, click More on the top toolbar → Chat / Participants.

Advanced sharing

Click Share → the Advanced tab. These options cover the situations the Basic tab can't.

OptionWhen to use itNotes
Portion of ScreenYou want to show only part of a window — for example one paragraph of a document, or a video player without the website around it.A green rectangle appears; drag its edges to frame exactly what the participants should see. Everything outside the rectangle stays private.
Computer Audio (audio only)Playing music or a recording with no picture.Your video stays on and large; the participants hear the sound clearly. Much better than sharing a music app's window.
Video (share a video file)Playing a saved video file from your computer.Zoom plays the file in its own player with smooth motion and sound. Preferred over sharing a media-player window.
Slides as Virtual BackgroundA PowerPoint or Keynote file you want to present with yourself in front of it.Your video appears in the corner over the slides. Looks polished; needs a plain background behind you.
Content from 2nd CameraShowing a book, a printed page, or something on your table through a second camera or document camera.Only appears if a second camera is connected.
WhiteboardWriting or drawing live — a quick sketch, a brainstorm.Found on the main toolbar as Whiteboards or in the Basic tab. Participants can draw too if you allow it.

Share settings the host should set (once, before the session)

  1. Click Security → make sure Share Screen is unticked for participants. Only the host and co-hosts can share.
  2. Click the ^ next to Share → Advanced Sharing Options… → under "Who can share?" choose Host and Co-hosts. Leave "One participant can share at a time".
  3. In the same place, "Who can start sharing when someone else is sharing?" → Host and Co-hosts, so one co-host can take over from another smoothly.

Windows

Shortcut to start/stop sharing: Alt + S. Pause/resume share: Alt + T. If a shared window shows black, turn off screen-dimming or "night light" overlay apps, or share the whole screen instead.

Mac

Shortcut: ⌘ Cmd + Shift + S. Pause/resume: ⌘ Cmd + Shift + T. The first time you share, macOS asks for Screen Recording permission: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording → switch Zoom on, then quit and reopen Zoom. Sharing computer sound may also ask to install a small audio helper — click Install.

Rehearse the shareOpen a test meeting on your own and share each file you plan to use. Check sound plays, and that nothing private is visible. Ten minutes of rehearsal saves a flustered five minutes in front of the participants.

12. When something goes wrong Stay calm, say what you are doing out loud, and try these in order.

ProblemTry this
Nobody can hear meAre you muted? Click Unmute. Then click the ^ next to it and pick the right microphone. If still silent, click Leave Computer Audio → Join Audio again.
I can't hear anyoneCheck your computer volume and that headphones are plugged in properly. Click ^ next to Mute → choose the right speaker → "Test Speaker".
My video is black or "cannot start video"Close other apps using the camera. Check the camera privacy setting (Section 3). On a laptop, check for a physical camera cover or slider.
Echo or feedbackSomeone has two devices unmuted in the same room, or a speaker too close to a mic. Ask everyone to use headphones; mute all.
Video is choppy or freezesTurn off your own video for a minute, close downloads and other video apps, move closer to the Wi-Fi router, or plug in a network cable.
I got disconnectedClick the meeting link again. Zoom will usually bring you back as host. If a co-host became host, ask them to click Participants → your name → More → Make Host.
A participant is stuck in the waiting roomParticipants → Waiting Room → Admit. If the meeting is locked, Security → untick Lock Meeting first.
A participant can't unmuteYou have "Unmute Themselves" switched off. Hover their name → Ask to Unmute, or Security → tick Unmute Themselves.
Can't find a buttonMake the Zoom window bigger, or click More (three dots) at the right end of the toolbar — hidden buttons are inside.
The toolbar disappearedMove your mouse — it hides after a few seconds. Press Alt (Windows) or Ctrl + \ (Mac) to show it, or Esc to leave full screen.
Everything has gone wrongSay "One moment, I'm fixing something" so everyone knows. Ask your co-host to take over. Quit Zoom fully and rejoin.

Session checklist

  • Zoom updated the day before
  • Joined 10 minutes early, mic and camera tested
  • Renamed to a name people recognise
  • Waiting room on; a co-host assigned
  • Chat set to "Hosts and co-hosts"; one-to-one chat off
  • Annotation off for participants
  • Participant screen share off
  • Hand-raising routine explained
  • Files to share open and rehearsed once
  • Breakout rooms: co-hosts assigned, return to main room allowed
  • Lock meeting after everyone has arrived
  • Save chat; End Meeting for All

And rememberLeave Room = back to the main room. Leave Meeting = gone. Read before you click.

Steps are written for the Zoom desktop app on Windows and macOS; the mobile and browser versions look different. If a label on your screen does not match, look in the same area of the toolbar or under More.
